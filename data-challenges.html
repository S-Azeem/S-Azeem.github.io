The MOT test reform

In 2018 the MOT test changed to follow the EU Roadworthiness directive (2014/45/EU). This means that the MOT test is now more stringent, and cars are more likely to fail, meaning there'd be a leniency bias in the old MOT tests. The data in the failure prediction model is from 2018 onwards, so it reflects the new test standards and the target variable is free of bias over the sample window.

The problem

Unfortunately there is no standardised format when entering data into the DVSA MOT Database for an MOT tester. This means that data is often none-uniform across records, some test centres specify engine codes, e.g., 318i and some are simpley keyed as 3 Series, making aggregations harder and thus a difficulty in deriving meaningful insight from the masses of data (a messy time mapping data...).

Often MOT testers also fail to verify the units the odometer measures in causing incoherent odometer reading (mileage) lineage, making the clockback detection very messy.

DVSA Wishlist

  • Unfortunately, there are no identifyers in the data showing which MOT centre the vehicle was tested at, meaning we can't pinpoint which MOT centres struggle to select the correct unit when entering an odometer unit nor can we figure out which MOT centres have suspiciously low failure rates...
  • It would be helpful to have use and history flags. Personal or fleet use and number of keepers. It would be interesting to see if there was a confirmed link in MOT defects as the keeper count increased or even between varying use cases.
  • It would give a better understanding of vehicle build quality (survival analysis) if there was a flag indicating why a vehicle had dropped out of the sample. Scrapped, exported, SORN or just untested. Right now a car that stops appearing could be any of these, which makes it hard to model how long cars actually last.
